# Voice Bible II: The World's Register (0.6)

Status: owner-approved 2026-08-05.

**This extends `docs/plans/0.5_voice_bible.md`. It supersedes nothing.** The
0.5 bible remains binding in full: its world voice, its two content rules,
its content boundary, and all fourteen cast entries. Read it first. This
document does three things the 0.5 bible left open:

1. Names the theme the cast is already expressing, so it can be applied
   deliberately instead of by instinct.
2. Extends that theme to **narration** (item, game, service, environment,
   and event description), which 0.5 left as a single flat voice.
3. Adds the **period methodology** and a **measurable brevity rule**, neither
   of which 0.5 specifies.

---

## Assessment: what the 0.5 bible is already doing

The bible's stated world voice is "neo-noir on a neon budget… a rain-slick,
late-night gambling town where the future arrived but the vices didn't
change." That is already the setting: a culture that did not move on, with
technology that did. Nothing about it needs revision. What follows makes it
specific.

The cast is more structured than it looks. Sorted by who employs them, a
pattern is exact:

**The house frames surveillance and control as hospitality.**
- Vivienne's written tic is literally "frames surveillance as hospitality";
  "A small courtesy first. We find gratitude improves attention."
- Rourke: "The cameras love you. So do I." Polite menace, "an accountant of
  consequences," never raises his voice.
- Casino Host: "Velvet over a velvet rope. Every kindness has a hook."
- Linda sits on the seam: house employee, warmth delivered through barred
  glass: "I didn't see a thing. I never do."

**The street treats the same machinery as a threat, and says so flatly.**
- Street Lender: "Fast cash. Your name in the book."
- Rival Counter: "Keep 'em where the cameras can't fall in love."
- Sal: transactional, lowballs, no courtesy layer at all.
- Snitch: "I know things. I sell things. Same thing, really."
- June, Motel Friend, the Brother-in-Law: warmth with no polish on it.

That split is not incidental. It is the bible's actual theme, and it has
never been written down.

## The theme, named

**Courtesy is how power gets expressed. Bluntness is how powerlessness gets
expressed.**

The house can afford to be gracious about what it takes, and being gracious
is part of taking it. The street cannot afford the dressing, so it says the
thing. Same machine, same transaction, opposite sentence.

The 0.5 cast already performs this. Narration does not yet participate.

## The gap

Every narration string in `data/` currently speaks in one uniform voice:
terse two-beat noir, identical rhythm whether it describes a gas-station
pull-tab or the Grand Casino floor. It is good writing that does not know
which side of the theme it is on.

~1,483 strings, ~34,500 characters. Average 23 characters. The corpus is
small enough that giving it the register split is a writing pass, not a
rebuild.

## Extension 1: The period methodology

The 0.5 bible establishes the *when* ("the future arrived but the vices
didn't change") but gives no rule for handling it on the page. These three
rules are that rule. They apply to narration and to cast lines equally.

**This is a methodology, not a costume.** No period slang, no pastiche, no
"old-timey" phrasing. The past comes through sentence construction. The
future comes through implication only.

**1. Plain declaratives, concrete nouns, no slang from any era.**
Slang reads as costume and dates the writing. Period feel comes from
unadorned construction: short, unhedged, physical. Nothing should sound
old. It should sound plain. This sharpens 0.5's "never purple, never a
manual" into something checkable.

**2. Nobody marvels at the future.**
No one in 1962 was amazed by their refrigerator. Advanced tech appears only
as a mundane convenience or a mundane annoyance. Never admired, never
explained, never called technology. This is the mechanism behind "the vices
didn't change": the tech is furniture, and furniture doesn't get a speech.

**3. Name the effect, not the device.**
Not "surveillance system," not "the house eye." Write *"the ceiling counts."* The
player assembles the tech themselves and it lands harder. This is 0.5's
"show the vibe, imply the use" applied to technology instead of items.

### Renamed nouns

Effect-first (rule 3) is always preferred. Use these only when the thing
must be named. Do not extend this list without an owner decision; if a
concept isn't covered, describe its effect in plain words.

| The thing | What they call it |
|---|---|
| surveillance / monitoring | the ceiling, the floorman |
| biometric scan / ID check | getting your picture took |
| data, records, history | the file, the book, the tape |
| networked comms | the line |
| drone | a little bird |
| predictive odds engine | the arithmetic |
| implant / worn device | a gadget |

"The book" is already Street Lender's ("your name in the book"). It is in
this table because the cast put it there.

## Extension 2: The brevity rule

0.5 asks for "economical" and "short sentences carry more menace than long
ones," which is direction without a measure. Here is the measure.

**A rewritten string may not exceed its original by more than 20%
characters.** The corpus averages 23 characters at two beats; that is the
house style and it is already right.

House register is *gracious*, not verbose. Street is *blunt*, not clipped
into nonsense. Character and context must survive inside the budget. If a
line needs more room than that, the line is wrong, not the budget.

## Extension 3: Narration registers

The cast has registers. Narration now does too, drawn from the same theme.

### House: control expressed as courtesy

- Machines take verbs of service: *keeps, provides, sees to, looks after*
- Passive voice for anything unpleasant: *guests are asked, the matter is
  handled*
- Machines may take warm adjectives: *careful, attentive, exact*
- Courteous rhythm, still short

### Street: control expressed as imposition

- Machines take verbs of taking: *counts, remembers, doesn't forget*
- Active, blunt, short
- Machines take **no** adjectives; they just do things
- Resentment lives in what is withheld. Never complain outright.

### Same noun, both ways

| | House | Street |
|---|---|---|
| the ceiling | "The ceiling keeps the room pleasant." | "The ceiling counts. Never wrong, never fair." |
| the arithmetic | "The arithmetic is checked nightly." | "The arithmetic knew before you sat down." |
| the file | "Your file is kept discreetly." | "There's a file. You've never seen it." |
| the cage | "The cage counts fast and exact." | "The cage counts. That's all it does for you." |

Note the House column is recognisably Vivienne's grammar and the Street
column is recognisably Sal's. That is the check: **if a narration line
couldn't plausibly sit next to that venue's characters, the register is
wrong.**

## Register assignment

Venues and themes are set. Nothing is renamed. Register follows the existing
archetype list and the cast already stationed there:

All eighteen archetypes are assigned. None are unclassified.

- **Street** (6): `corner_store`, `back_alley`, `motel`, `bar`,
  `gas_station_casino`, `pawn_shop` *(Sal, June, Motel Friend)*
- **House** (6): `grand_casino`, `grand_casino_high_limit`,
  `grand_casino_back_room`, `grand_casino_cage`, `delta_queen`,
  `kitty_cat_lounge` *(Vivienne, Rourke, Linda, Casino Host)*
- **Seam** (3): `small_underground_casino`, `jazz_club`, `beach`
- **Private** (3): `motel_room`, `apartment`, `house` *(kind: `home`)*

**Seam is not a third register.** It is House grammar on Street specifics:
the room reaching for courtesy it cannot fully afford. Use House verbs on
objects that are visibly worn. Linda is the cast model for it.

**Private is genuinely outside both.** The three `home` archetypes are the
only rooms nobody else owns. No ceiling counts here, no courtesy is extended,
nothing is being taken. Write them plain and quiet, with no watching verbs at
all and no machine as a grammatical subject. The existing lines already do
this: "A rented pause.", "Better locks. Softer silence.", "Your roof. For
now." Private sits outside the theme the way Pal and Dave do, and that
contrast is the point: it is the only place the machinery is absent.

`grand_casino_back_room` is Rourke's room and takes the purest House voice in
the game. `grand_casino_cage` is Linda's and is the one House room written at
the Seam: house grammar delivered through barred glass.

## Register is decided by the union of a string's venues

A string does not take the register of the venue you happened to read it
from. It takes the register of **every** venue that pools it. If that set
spans more than one register, the string must be written **neutral**.

This is computable from the data. Take the union of all `event_pool` or
`service_pool` memberships across `archetypes.json`, map each venue to its
register above, and if the resulting set has more than one member, the string
is neutral. It is not a judgment call.

Worked cases from the existing data:

| string | venues span | register |
|---|---|---|
| `late_shift_discount` | Street only | Street |
| `eye_in_the_sky` | House only | House |
| `jazz_trio_set_break` | Seam only | Seam |
| `rival_counter` | House + Seam | neutral |
| `door_bribe` | Street + Seam + House | neutral |
| `house_drink` | Street + Seam + House | neutral |

Events belonging to **no** venue pool at all (game-attached probes,
tutorial hooks, bus encounters) are likewise neutral, since they can surface
anywhere.

This rule is retroactive: the 0.6 calibration pilot got it wrong once, by
assigning register from the pool it was read out of. `door_bribe` was written
in House courtesy and appeared at a gas station. Corrected on 2026-08-05.

## The width rule

Beyond the +20% per-string budget, a rewritten string **may not exceed the
longest pre-existing string of its own field kind** in that file (item
`description`, event `start_summary`, and so on).

The budget protects rhythm. This protects layout. No panel in this game has
ever had to render an item description longer than 41 characters, and a
writing pass is not the place to find out what happens at 47. Compute the
per-field maximum before writing and treat it as a hard ceiling.

## Clarifications to 0.5, not overrides

**The player speaks, but their words are never shown.** The player character
talks: they ask, haggle, refuse, and negotiate out loud in the fiction. The
player simply never sees the line and never learns exactly what was said.

This is a narration permission, not a silence. Narration may freely report
that the player spoke and what it accomplished. "A word passes. He owes you
a quiet one." and "You come away with a cleaner read on the rooms." are both
correct: speech happened, the wording stayed off-screen. What is banned is
surfacing the actual line, in quotes or paraphrase specific enough to read as
a quote.

0.5's application note ("event choice labels get
personality and stop announcing their mechanics (→ the character's actual
words") applies to NPC lines. For player choice labels it means personality
*in the intent phrasing*, never quoted player speech.

The existing data already does this correctly: `Haggle`, `Move On`,
`Cash In`, `Ask too quietly`, `Trade for a route`, `Enough for now`. Every
one has personality and none is speech. `Ask too quietly` is the model.
Preserve this. Do not convert a label into a quoted line.

**0.5's two content rules stand unchanged.** Pools of 3+ lines per recurring
speaker; tooltips brief, characterful, never a spec. The register split
applies *within* a pool, and a pool doesn't mix registers.

**0.5's content boundary stands unchanged.** Seedy noir, not cruelty.

## Cross-tier strings

`blackjack`, `slot`, and `video_poker` each appear at tiers 1, 2, and 3;
`pull_tabs` at 1 and 3; 15 items are `domain: global`. One string must serve
a gas station and the Grand Casino.

**Write these register-neutral and let the venue carry the color.** Every
archetype already has its own `objective_hint` and visual description, and
events, services, and items are pooled per-venue, so register falls out of
where a string already lives, at no cost. The ~30 genuinely global strings
are written flat and inherit register from the room around them.

```
gas station  →  "Buy in, peel, cash out before the counter looks up."   [street]
blackjack    →  "Beat twenty-one without becoming worth watching."      [neutral]
grand casino →  "The room is pleased to have you. It's also counting."  [house]
```

Per-tier overrides are **not** in scope, since they would be a schema change.
Revisit only if the neutral middle reads flat at length.

## Owner decisions, locked 2026-08-05

**Dialogue is in scope.** Character speech is rewritten alongside narration:
`voice.lines` in `characters.json` (202 strings) and `nodes[*].text` in
`dialogues.json` (73 strings). This amends the earlier statement that cast
speech was out of scope.

**Character definitions and personalities are not in scope.** `voice.style`,
`display_name`, `title`, and `role` stay exactly as written, and every
character keeps their 0.5 bible characterisation. What they say is rewritten;
who they are is not.

**Neutral leans Street, by design.** The objects and rooms neutral describes
are mostly worn and cheap, and the player carries them up from the street. A
true-center neutral reads bloodless. This is settled and should not be
re-litigated per pass.

**Clarity outranks atmosphere.** Where a line is opaque about what is
happening or what a choice does, the rewrite makes it legible. Mechanical
meaning, slot, and intent are preserved; murky prose is not.

## The 0.5 pass shipped, then lost its apostrophes

The 0.5 writing pass executed on 2026-07-29 in commit `1c813562`, rewriting
all 15 data files. It landed: the bible's own example lines are live in the
data, and 68 line pools exist.

Two things went wrong afterward, both binding on this pass.

**1. Apostrophes were stripped.** The approved bible lines and the shipped
data no longer match:

```
bible:  "Casino chips. They wouldn't cash 'em and you bring 'em to me. Cute."
data:   "Casino chips. They would not cash them and you bring them to me. Cute."
```

Measured across `data/`: `do not` 15 vs `don't` 2, `it is` 15 vs `it's` 1.
The bible is the reverse. 41 strings carry formal expansions, concentrated in
`characters.json` (28) and `dialogues.json` (7). Elisions went too: the bible
uses `'em` four times, the data once.

Contraction-free speech reads stiff and formal, which is the "old-timey
costume" this document forbids. **Restoring contractions and elisions is
binding on every future pass** and does not count against the brevity budget,
since it is restoration toward the approved bible rather than new writing.
Exception by judgment: leave the formal form where it is in character, as it
may be for Rourke.

No sanitizer, hook, or validator rule capable of re-stripping apostrophes
exists in the repo, so the restoration will hold. The data also contains zero
BBCode and the UI does not use `RichTextLabel`, so the bible's occasional
italic emphasis cannot be carried as markup; carry it in word order.

**2. The code half never ran.** The archived prompt
(`docs/todone/writing_voice_pass_prompt.md`) called hardcoded strings in
`scripts/games/*.gd` "a LARGE body, do not skip." Commit `1c813562` touched
zero `.gd` files. A rough estimate puts ~2,100 sentence-like player-facing
strings in non-test code (`scripts/ui` ~920, `scripts/games` ~683,
`scripts/core` ~498), none of which any voice pass has touched. That body is
**not** in the 0.6 rollout scope either and needs its own decision.

## Placement has two mechanisms, not one

Register is the union of **both** `event_pool` / `service_pool` membership
**and** the entry's own `scopes`, matched against each archetype's
`event_scopes`. The earlier version of this document accounted only for
pools, which is why `high_roller_cashout` (no pool, `scopes: ["boss"]`, so
House) would have been mis-registered as neutral.

| scope | reaches | register |
|---|---|---|
| `boss` | 4 grand casino rooms | House |
| `home` | the 3 home archetypes | Private |
| `meta` | pawn_shop | Street |
| `recovery` | beach | Seam |
| `shop` | back_alley, corner_store, jazz_club, motel, pawn_shop | neutral (Seam+Street) |
| `casino` | bar, delta_queen, gas_station_casino, kitty_cat_lounge, small_underground_casino | neutral (all three) |
| `any` | everything | neutral |

Where an entry belongs to a named character, **the character's register wins
over the placement register.** Placement is for rooms; characters are people,
and their event narration renders directly beside their own dialogue.

## Constraints for any pass run off this document

1. The player speaks but their words are never shown; narration may report
   that they spoke, never what they said. Choice labels stay intent-shaped.
2. Meaning is preserved. Reword, don't rewrite. Same content, same slot,
   same mechanical implication.
3. No renaming. Venues, characters, games, items, themes are set.
4. No numbers as prose (0.5 rule, unchanged).
5. No schema changes.
6. Cast dialogue is governed by 0.5 §The cast. This document governs
   narration around them.
