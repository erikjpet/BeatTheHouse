# Beat the House — Voice Bible (0.5)

Status: owner-approved and binding for the 0.5 writing pass
(`docs/todo/writing_voice_pass_prompt.md` Phase 2) and all future copy. It
supersedes the flatter, functional tone in the current data.

## The world's voice (narration, item & environment descriptions, tooltips)

Neo-noir on a neon budget. A rain-slick, late-night gambling town where the
future arrived but the vices didn't change. The narrator has watched a
hundred players walk in hot and limp out broke, and it shows: **dry,
knowing, a little seedy, economical.** Never purple, never a manual.

- Show the vibe, imply the use. Describe what a thing *is to you*, not a
  spec of what it does.
- Second person or terse third. Short sentences carry more menace than long
  ones.
- No robot exposition. "Grants +2 luck for 3 turns" is banned as prose; the
  glyphs/badges carry the numbers, the words carry the flavor.

**Before → after feel:**
- "The lender counts out fast cash and writes your name down." → keep the
  cold, cut the flab: "Fast cash. Your name in the book. Don't lose the
  book's number." (Street Lender voice, below.)
- Item desc "Protects pawn loans." → "Keeps Sal honest. Or honest enough."

## The two content rules (codify into the style guide)

1. **Multiple lines, always.** Every recurring speaker and every repeatable
   action draws from a POOL of at least 3 lines, shown without immediate
   repetition. Characters must never say the same thing twice in a row.
   Expand every single-line response in the data into a pool.
2. **Tooltips: brief, descriptive, characterful.** One short evocative line.
   Convey the vibe and the gist — NOT a word-for-word list of effects. If a
   player needs the exact number, that's what the glyph/badge is for. Cut
   every tooltip to its essence.

## Content boundary

Edgy, dry, rude-in-character is the point. Keep it tasteful and in-theme —
seedy noir, not cruelty. No slurs, no punching down, nothing that reads as
genuinely offensive rather than world-weary.

## The cast

### Rourke — the Pit Boss (Grand Casino)

The house's certainty made flesh. Calm, watchful, never raises his voice
because he has never once needed to. Reads you like a marked deck. Polite
menace — the scary part is how *reasonable* he sounds. Not a villain; worse,
an accountant of consequences.
- **Tics:** short weighted sentences; states outcomes as inevitable; never
  uses your name; "I'm not angry" energy.
- **Lines:**
  - "You've been busy. I like to know who's busy on my floor."
  - "Sit. This won't take long — one way or the other."
  - "Everybody's got a system. Yours just happens to be theft."
  - "I'm not angry. Angry's for amateurs. I'm certain."
  - "The cameras love you. So do I. Come see the back room."

### Linda — the Cage (Players Card, cashout, comps)

A dark silhouette behind the barred glass; you never see her face and she
likes it that way. Warm, knowing, maternal-but-not-soft — she's seen
everyone and roots for you anyway, or does a flawless impression of it.
Leans into her own mystery with dry affection.
- **Tics:** pet names ("sugar," "honey," "sweetheart"); talks like she's
  slipping you a small secret favor; never confirms what she looks like.
- **Lines:**
  - "Slide it under the glass, sugar. I don't bite. Much."
  - "Bronze already? Somebody's been behaving. Mostly."
  - "The house doesn't crown debtors, honey. Clear the marker, then we'll
    talk gold."
  - "I didn't see a thing. I never do." *(look-away comp)*
  - "Careful with that card, sweetheart. Lose the run holding it and it's
    gone. Like me — you'll never get a good look at it again."

### Sal — the Pawn Shop

A fence with a shelf and a grudge against your taste. Gruff, mocking,
transactional, and — under it all — a little fond of you, the way you're
fond of a bad habit. Lowballs everything and calls it generosity.
- **Tics:** insults the goods; quotes low; "generous"; needles you for
  coming back.
- **Lines:**
  - "This? I'll give you tin and call myself generous."
  - "Back already. The shelf missed you. It's the only thing that did."
  - "Buy it back if you want. Costs more than you sold it. That's the
    business, friend."
  - "Casino chips. They wouldn't cash 'em and you bring 'em to *me*. Cute."

### The Crew — favor lender

A "we," never an "I." Friendly the way a wolf is friendly. They hand you
cash like it was always yours, because soon enough your time will be theirs.
- **Tics:** collective voice; implies the debt is already owed; favors, not
  interest.
- **Lines:**
  - "The Crew hands it over like it was always yours. It wasn't. Remember
    that."
  - "We don't do interest. We do favors. You'll see which is worse."
  - "Take the money. Take your time. We're patient. We're never gone."

### Your Brother-in-Law — family lender

Guilt is the interest rate. Passive-aggressive, sighs a lot, will absolutely
bring this up at dinner. Loves your spouse; tolerates you.
- **Tics:** "family helps family"; references the wedding/your marriage;
  the long-suffering sigh.
- **Lines:**
  - "Family helps family. I'll just mention it at every dinner. Forever."
  - "For my sister's sake. Not yours. Let's be clear about that."
  - "Of course. Of course. Don't worry about paying it back. *(He will
    worry about it.)*"

### Street Lender — cash, no sentiment

Cold and brief. Money, name, book, done. The kindness is that he's fast.
- **Lines:**
  - "Fast cash. Your name in the book. Don't make me look you up."
  - "No sob stories. I've got the whole shelf of 'em."

### Motel Friend — low-rent buddy

Spots you a little; it's nothing; it's a little something; it's not nothing.
Warm, broke, dependable until he isn't.
- **Lines:**
  - "I got you. It's nothing. It's a little something. It's not nothing."
  - "Pay me when you're flush. You'll be flush. Right? Right."

### June — the bartender

Pours strong, knows everyone's business, and hands it out with the drinks.
World-weary warmth; a running commentary on your night.
- **Tics:** calls your order "the usual" the second time; gossip; gentle
  warnings she knows you'll ignore.
- **Lines:**
  - "The usual? 'Course it is. You've got a usual now. That's how it
    starts."
  - "Drink slow. Or don't. Your funeral, your tab."
  - "Heard the pit boss is in tonight. Just saying. To my glasses. Loudly."

### Dealers (generic, per venue; rotate daily)

Professional, dry, table-bound. Terse call-and-response. A fresh dealer is a
fresh, slightly cooler read on you.
- **Lines:**
  - "Place it or lose it, friend."
  - "House pays to seventeen. House always pays to seventeen."
  - "New face at the felt tonight. That'd be me."

### Casino Host / After-Hours Host

Smooth, comped smile, wants you loose and playing. Velvet over a velvet
rope. Every kindness has a hook.
- **Lines:**
  - "Anything you need. Anything at all. The house insists."
  - "Another drink? On us. Everything's on us. That's the idea."

### Dave — the bus encounter (one-time)

A stranger on the night bus who knows things he has no business knowing.
Mystical, sparse, serene — almost a wizard in a windbreaker. He speaks
rarely, and when he does it lands like the smartest thing you've ever heard,
then he goes quiet again. Part oracle, part hobo prophet. Never explains
himself; never needs to.
- **Tics:** few words; unnerving calm; cryptic truths that turn out exactly
  right; comfortable silences that say more than the sentence did.
- **Lines:**
  - "You're chasing the money. The money's chasing something too. Ask it
    what."
  - "The house always wins. Unless it forgets to. It forgets more than
    you'd think."
  - "You'll know when to walk away. You'll ignore it. Everyone does."
  - "Same bus. Same faces. Different names. You're doing fine." *(He does not
    elaborate. He never elaborates.)*

### Patron archetypes

- **Suspicious Patron** — paranoid, coat, whispers a tip and immediately
  regrets it. "You didn't hear it from me. You didn't hear anything. Corner
  table. Forget I said corner table."
- **Braggart / Rowdy Regular** — loud, won once, will tell you about it.
  "I hit it big here. Once. Best night of my — you buying? I'm buying. Are
  you buying?"
- **Whale / Velvet Regular** — rich, bored, dangerous to imitate. "Money's
  just chips with better lighting, friend."
- **Rival Counter** — a fellow cheat; wary professional kinship. "Nice
  hands. Keep 'em where the cameras can't fall in love."
- **Snitch** — nervous, sells to both sides. "I know things. I sell things.
  Same thing, really."
- **The Collector** — comes calm for what you owe. "No rush. Debts don't
  spoil. I do the waiting so you don't have to."
- **Unknown Caller** — ominous, brief, a phone in the dark. "You know who
  this is. You know what this is about."

## Application notes for the writing pass

- Every one of the above is a POOL to expand — write 3-5 lines per
  character per repeated beat (greet, deal, win, loss, borrow, sell,
  ambient), not one.
- Event choice labels get personality and stop announcing their mechanics
  ("A riskier route opens" → the character's actual words); the consequence
  still fires in data.
- Environment descriptions, objective hints, and suspicion cues move into
  the narrator's neo-noir voice.
- Tooltips get the brief-and-characterful treatment across items, icons,
  and controls.
