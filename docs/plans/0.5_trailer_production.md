# Beat the House 0.5 trailer production

Produced 2026-07-26 from the Godot 4.6 project in
`D:\Projects\Beat-The-House`. The master is a 60-second gameplay trailer,
supported by a 45-second vertical cut and an 8-second silent page loop. Every
gameplay frame and every sound in the audio bed came from this project.

## Research and edit philosophy

The edit follows four practical findings:

1. **Gameplay and genre must read immediately.** Steam warns that store users
   may give a trailer less than ten seconds and explicitly recommends putting
   gameplay first, keeping the HUD visible, and making the premise legible
   without audio. The trailer therefore cold-opens on Rourke's live blackjack
   table, before any logo. Source:
   [Steamworks trailer best practices](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer?l=english).
2. **Sell genre, then hook, then range.** Derek Lieu's genre → hook → content
   order argues against leading with feature-count cards before the viewer
   knows what kind of game they are watching. The first four seconds establish
   casino play, heat, a boss, and the title; the eight-game card only arrives
   after the roadside world is visible. Source:
   [Game Trailer Structure — Genre, Hook, Content](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2021/4/12/game-trailer-structure-genre-hook-content).
3. **Title cards name an idea; gameplay proves it.** The cards are brief
   signposts, not explanations. "EIGHT CASINO GAMES", "DODGE THE HEAT", and
   "CHEAT IF YOU DARE" are followed by real footage of those ideas. Source:
   [Simple Game Trailer Templates](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/3/1/simple-game-trailer-templates).
4. **Rhythm should support clarity.** Cuts and actions land on musical beats
   where possible, but the montage never implies that Beat the House is a
   rhythm game. Lieu recommends beat-synced cuts and actions once the footage
   remains comprehensible, and warns against style obscuring the mechanics.
   Sources:
   [Trailer Music Video Style Editing](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2019/11/26/trailer-music-video-style-editing) and
   [How to Think About Pacing in a Trailer](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/11/16/how-to-think-about-pacing-in-a-trailer).

Resulting edit rules:

- Give the first three seconds to a tense, readable gameplay problem.
- Use hard cuts; do not dissolve unrelated game states together.
- Keep the HUD because bankroll, heat, intoxication, and time are the game.
- Hold a shot only until its action or idea reads.
- Use one- to five-second sections and accelerate to 1.5-second game shots.
- Cut in 0.5-second multiples against the Jazz Club's 120 BPM pulse.
- Use cards for pillar names only, with large type and safe margins.
- Let the game score carry the whole edit instead of splicing environment
  music resets or adding external trailer music.
- End with a readable platform and location, not a logo alone.

## Music decision

The audio bed is the game's own dynamically generated Jazz Club arrangement.
Its data-authored 120 BPM dwell arc is already shaped like a trailer cue:
sparse → bass build → drum build → peak → release → rebuild, in four-bar
blocks. `tools/trailer_capture.gd --segment=music_bed` renders 76.5 seconds
through Godot Movie Maker; the edit takes 60 seconds after an eight-second
generation warm-up, normalizes it to approximately -16 LUFS, and applies only
short boundary fades.

Using one in-game capture avoids audible score resets between the sixteen
separate deterministic visual captures. Gameplay SFX were not mixed over the
bed because their captured files also contain different environment music;
mixing those tracks would make the score phase and clash. No external music,
stock audio, or synthesized trailer effects are present.

## Reproduction

Prerequisites:

- Godot:
  `.tools/godot-4.6-stable/Godot_v4.6-stable_win64_console.exe`
- FFmpeg and FFprobe on `PATH`, or the Gyan FFmpeg winget package
- Python with Pillow

Install the encoder if needed:

```powershell
winget install --id Gyan.FFmpeg --exact --accept-source-agreements --accept-package-agreements
```

Render every raw segment, regenerate cards, assemble the master/vertical/loop,
and print final probes:

```powershell
Set-Location D:\Projects\Beat-The-House
.\tools\validate_project.ps1
.\tools\render_trailer.ps1
```

Reuse existing `.tmp/trailer/raw/*.avi` captures while rebuilding the edit:

```powershell
.\tools\render_trailer.ps1 -SkipCapture -Force
```

Delete or move a single stale raw AVI and rerun without `-Force` to recapture
only that missing segment. `-Force` recaptures every raw segment unless
`-SkipCapture` is also supplied.

The script performs these operations:

1. `tools/generate_trailer_cards.py` writes 16:9 and 9:16 PNG cards.
2. Godot Movie Maker calls `tools/trailer_capture.gd` once per fixed-seed
   segment at 60 FPS, with audio captured into MJPEG/PCM AVIs.
3. FFmpeg trims from the logged active frame and scales 1280×720 gameplay to
   1920×1080 using `flags=neighbor`.
4. Identically encoded H.264 clips are concatenated with hard cuts.
5. The in-game music capture is trimmed, normalized, and encoded as 48 kHz
   stereo AAC at 192 kbps.
6. The vertical cut uses source-aware 405×720 crops and nearest-neighbor
   scaling to 1080×1920.
7. The loop is encoded as silent VP9 WebM.

Heavy AVIs, intermediate clips, concat lists, and QA contact sheets stay in
`.tmp/trailer/` and are not committed.
