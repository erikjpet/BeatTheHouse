# 0.6 Roadmap — The Living Town & The Crew

Status: **v4 — OWNER-APPROVED FOUNDATION** (2026-08-13). Four rounds of
owner decisions are locked and integrated. This document is the binding
design contract for the 0.6 implementation queue at
`docs/todo/README_0_6_board.md`. Nothing here is implemented yet.

## Vision

0.5 finished the machine. 0.6 makes the town alive.

Today a run randomizes *topology* — which order you see the environments in —
but never *life*. The bar is always the same three machines under the same
neon with the same 2–3 events rolled from one pool. 0.6's promise:

> **Every time the player walks into an environment, something is happening
> there tonight.** A wake at the bar. A wedding charter on the Delta Queen.
> A police sweep working its way across town, one node ahead of you. And the
> environments are not twelve pockets — they are one town on one night, and
> the town talks.

The second promise is the flagship rabbithole:

> **The Crew stops being lender #3 and becomes a run-defining path.** Seven
> already-authored characters — Wheelman, Fixer, Collector, Scout, Planner,
> Inside Man, Runner — become people you meet, earn, and finally take into
> the Grand Casino for a score, as a full alternative to the solo showdown.

# Pillar 1 — The Tonight System (living environments)

Every archetype gains a pool of **scenarios**: what is happening at this
venue tonight. Exactly one scenario is seeded per node at first visit and
stored in the node's environment snapshot. Scenarios may have **phases**
that advance at action boundaries (never wall-clock), so a party found at
its peak is winding down when you return six turns later.

### What a scenario is allowed to touch

A scenario is a data-driven overlay on the archetype, never a new room:

| Axis | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Patron/staff set | Which named NPCs and extras are present |
| Event pool | Adds scenario-exclusive events, removes ill-fitting ones |
| Economic profile | Stake floor/ceiling shifts, cashout tone |
| Game modifiers | A rowdy table plays looser; a watched floor plays tighter |
| Services | Adds/removes (open bar, locked kitchen, private table) |
| Music profile | Overrides the archetype profile (system already per-venue) |
| Presentation | Palette tint, lighting, crowd density, signage line |
| Exclusive opportunity | One special offer/game/encounter unique to the night |
| Security | Strictness shifts, cheat-risk windows, machine alarm tolerance |
| Hooks | Crew recruitment placements, storyline-chain beats, rumors |

Schema lives in a new `data/environments/scenarios.json` keyed by archetype,
with weights, phase definitions, and mutation blocks. Selection is run-RNG
seeded and deterministic; challenge configs may pin or exclude scenarios
(instant tutorial/testing value).

### Launch density (owner-locked)

**3–4 scenarios per venue at launch, done well.** The catalog below is the
authored backlog; the launch cut selects the 3–4 strongest per venue
(marked ★), and the rest wait for a later content pass. Every launch
scenario ships complete: exclusive events, music/palette variant, and at
least one hook into another pillar.

same-archetype visits (rare) and repeat runs don't repeat scenarios.
Rare scenarios (The Wake, The Buyout, Engine Trouble) carry low weights and
memorable exclusives — the "remember the run where…" generator.

# Pillar 2 — One Town, One Night (the connected world)

A run-level **town state** that every environment reads, so the world feels
continuous instead of episodic.

### Town conditions

Seeded per run, some static, some evolving on turn boundaries:

- **Weather track**: clear → rain → fog → storm. Touches music, travel
  risk/cost bands, scenario weighting (Storm Coming, Fog Delay), palette,
  and Streets-run visibility (Pillar 3).
- **Calendar**: payday vs mid-week. Payday raises crowds and stakes
  everywhere at once; mid-week empties tier-1 venues.
- **Citywide happenings** (0–2 per run): *Fight Night* (bars packed,
  casino floors thin), *Festival Weekend* (beach/riverboat buffed),
  *Police Sweep* (below), *Rolling Blackout* (generalizes the existing
  `lights_out` event into a town condition; heist Plan D criteria hook).

### The Police Sweep (ships fully fleshed in 0.6.0 — owner-locked)

A first-class system, not an event:

- **The track.** When a run seeds a sweep (not every run has one), a
  sweep unit walks a deterministic path across the world-map graph, one
  node per N turns, seeded from run RNG. It prefers tier-1 → tier-2
  order and never enters the Grand Casino (the house handles its own).
- **Reading it.** The sweep is never shown as a marker for free. It is
  legible through: rumors ("cruisers were outside the Low Tide an hour
  ago"), scenario mutations (*Cruiser Parked*, *Serial-Check Day*, *Raid
  Jitters* auto-weight toward adjacent nodes), Switch's intel (crew path
  reveals the sweep's current node and heading on the map), and the map
  itself once you've personally seen it (marker persists with staleness).
- **Being caught in it.** If the sweep enters your node: a sweep
  encounter scales with heat, carried contraband (cheat items and
  Numbers slips become hot cargo), and active debts with the street.
  Outcomes range from a shakedown fee to item confiscation to a
  travel-lock — never an instant run-kill.
- **The wake.** Nodes the sweep just left get a "swept" window: security
  strictness drops a band, cheat windows open, pusher alarm tolerance
  loosens, the underground breathes. Reward for reading the board:
  gamble *behind* the sweep.
- **Crew interplay.** Knuckles can stash contraband before a sweep hits;
  Numbers collections pause on swept nodes; the Punchline's cover means
  the sweep passes layer 1 without finding layers 2–3 — unless town heat
  is extreme. Streets runs route *around* the sweep — or through it, for
  the brave.

### Rumors — environments talking about each other

A rumor system that surfaces *true* statements about other nodes' seeded
scenarios and conditions: the bartender mentions the wedding charter; a
trucker says the Punchline's door got mean. Mechanically, hearing a rumor
upgrades that node's map preview (the existing `scouted` flag pipeline —
already built, waiting). Rumors also carry **Numbers whispers** (hot
numbers, fix leaks — Pillar 3) and **pusher pile states** ("the pusher at
the gas station is *hanging*"). Rumors are the connective tissue that
makes scenarios feel like a town instead of a slot machine of vignettes.

### Traveling NPCs

A handful of characters with seeded itineraries who move between venues:
- **Dave** (exists) — the template: bus stories reference where he's been.
- **Cass Venn, rival counter** — works the same town you do; venues she hit
  before you are hotter, tables she's at now are opportunities or trouble.
- **Silas Crow, the snitch** — sells information both directions; where he
  drinks tonight determines who knows what about you.
- **Crew members** (Pillar 3) — met where their lives put them, not at a
  fixed kiosk.

### Consequences that follow you

Generalize the snitch/reputation seed: incidents propagate along map edges.
Trip a pusher alarm at the gas station and the corner store clerk watches
your hands; tip generously at the wake and the town hears you're good
people. Small, legible, deterministic — reputation as town gossip, not a
stat bar.

# Pillar 3 — The Crew Path (flagship)

**Philosophy:** the Crew is a *playstyle choice with a cost*, not extra
content everyone consumes. A run can stay solo (current game), lean house
(comps/host/Players Card), or go crew — trading independence and a cut of
everything for capability, protection, and a different ending. Per
round-3 lock: every crew system is optional and independent; they overlap
only through crew integration, never through requirements.

## The Punchline — the crew's house (owner-locked)

The `small_underground_casino` archetype is reworked into a **three-layer
venue the crew controls**: **The Punchline**.

- **Layer 1 — The comedy club (street level, public).** A legitimate,
  slightly desperate comedy club: stage, two-drink minimum, open-mic and
  headliner scenarios, terrible comics as ambient texture. It exists to
  be *boring to the law*. New players see a comedy club on the map and
  nothing else.
- **Layer 2 — The casino (hidden).** The existing underground casino
  content lives here, reached through the club (the shipped `side_door`
  event becomes the discovery seam: a password, a bribe, a regular's
  nod, or crew standing). Stakes and cheat-looseness as today, now with
  its own scenario set.
- **Layer 3 — The back room (crew only).** Behind the casino: the crew's
  hangout, revealed only through Rook / crew trust. Contains the job
  board, crew members in residence (varies by their itineraries), the
  back-room poker table, the Numbers desk (the racket's home office),
  the planning table for the heist, and crew-only services. The one room
  in town where nobody is watching you.

Layer navigation happens *inside* the environment (one map node, three
floors), so world-map identity, routes, and the guaranteed
underground → Grand Casino shortcut edge all survive intact. Each layer
has its own music profile — the club's tinny stage sound bleeding into
the casino's hush is the venue's signature. The crew being *in charge*
recolors everything: layer-2 security is "the crew's rules, not the
house's"; Debt Court is theirs; sweeps can't see past layer 1; hostile
acts in layers 2–3 damage crew trust directly.

## Trust ladder

Per-member trust plus crew-wide standing, earned only through play:

`Stranger → Marker (took the loan — today's system) → Associate (did jobs)
→ Made (layer 3 access, coordinated plays) → Inner Circle (heist-eligible)`

The existing favor system is retconned as the Marker rung — no shipped
behavior changes, it just stops being the ceiling.

## The Streets — the crew's playable town (round-3 mandate)

One new game-surface framework that turns "go somewhere carrying
something" into actual gameplay, used by package jobs, Numbers running,
and (optionally) heist legs.

**The surface.** Taking a run job opens a compact street board: a
stylized block map generated from the world-map edge you're traversing
(origin venue → destination venue, block count scaled from the edge's
distance band). Turn-based movement across intersections. The board is
seeded and deterministic; town state paints it:

- **Patrol cones** — cruisers with sweeping sightlines; the Police
  Sweep's proximity adds units; *Cruiser Parked* scenarios spill onto
  adjacent boards.
- **Light and weather** — rain shrinks sightlines (easier, slower); fog
  more so; blackout blocks are dark (fast, but something else is in the
  dark); clear payday nights are bright and busy.
- **Crowds** — Fight Night and Festival crowds block routes but break
  line-of-sight: slow cover.
- **Fixed props** — stash boxes (bank the package mid-route and come
  back), shortcut alleys (fast, exposed), the snitch's window (pass it
  and Silas knows your business).

**The verbs.** Each turn: move (walk = quiet, run = fast but noisy),
wait (let a cone swing), duck (into cover/crowd), stash, or ditch (dump
the package — job failed, evidence gone). Getting spotted starts a
pursuit timer rather than instant failure: make the destination or a
stash before it closes, or eat the consequence.

**The stakes.** Carrying a package applies contraband rules: spotted =
heat; caught = package confiscated, crew trust hit, and whatever was in
the package becomes tomorrow's problem. Clean fast runs pay bonuses and
trust; Rook teaches route tricks as trust rises; Lucky's runs (Numbers
routes) are multi-stop variants — several venues before the deadline,
route-ordering strategy included.

**Why it's a game and not a menu:** the board is where the living town
becomes *tactile* — you literally walk through tonight's weather, past
tonight's crowds, around tonight's sweep. The delivery jobs that today
resolve as `crew_favor_delivery` text become runs you play.

*(Cut-safe extras: The Lookout attention minigame folds into Streets as
a "hold position" job variant; the Practice Rig dice trainer stays a
layer-3 prop.)*

## Jobs

Offered via events and the layer-3 job board: package runs (Streets),
Numbers routes (Streets), lookout holds (Streets variant), stake-horse
plays (gamble crew money, split the win, eat the loss), collections
(Knuckles asks you to be the friendly face first). Jobs pay trust, cash,
and story; failing them costs trust — and unpaid trust debts feed The
Turn's ledger (below).

## Coordinated plays (crew in the casino with you)

With a Made member present in-venue: **Spotter** (Switch: counting boost
+ suspicion relief at blackjack), **Distraction** (Velvet/Lucky: dump
heat mid-session once), **Big Player call-in** (walk into a pre-warmed
shoe), **Chip Dump** (baccarat partner-play to move money quietly),
**Table Flood** (crew crowds the table — camouflage for a cheat window).
Each is a limited-use, seeded, visible-cost tool — not a passive buff.

## The Numbers (approved round 3 — expanded design)

The crew's street lottery, run from the layer-3 desk, played all over
town. Fully independent of the heist (owner-locked): you never need the
Numbers for the heist, and you never need the heist to play, run, or
cheat the Numbers.

### The game itself (anyone can play)

- **The draw.** One draw per in-game day, resolved at a fixed action
  boundary, seeded from run RNG. Diegetically the number is "the
  handle" — the last three digits of the published take at the track,
  posted each night. Nobody can pick the number; that's the racket's
  whole sales pitch.
- **Playing.** Buy a slip at any crew-touched venue (bar, gas station,
  corner store, Punchline, motel): pick three digits and a stake.
  Straight play pays long (capped); box play (any order) pays shorter.
  Slips are inventory items — contraband if the sweep finds them.
- **The rhythm.** The daily draw gives runs a heartbeat: the town talks
  about yesterday's number, hot numbers trend in rumor chatter (pure
  superstition — unless someone's fixed it, below).

### Runner mode (crew path, via Lucky)

Work a collection route before the draw closes: a multi-stop Streets run
— collect slips from three or four venues, order your route around the
sweep and the weather, and get the bag to the Punchline before post
time. Pays a percentage of the bag plus trust. Late = the crew eats
losses and you eat the blame. Swept while carrying = the worst version
of everything.

### Rigging it — two routes, one temptation

**The crew fix (crew path).** At high trust, Lucky and Mags cut you in
on how it really works: the handle is honest, but *the man who posts it
isn't*. The fix is an operation with player roles, not a menu click:

1. **The bribe run** — carry the envelope to the tote-board man's
   contact (a hard Streets run: the package is radioactive, the route is
   long).
2. **The camouflage** — before the fixed draw, place spread bets across
   town so the winning action doesn't point at one face (a route-and-
   allocation puzzle: too concentrated = obvious; too thin = smaller
   score).
3. **The payday** — the fixed number hits; **the crew pays you a cut
   for your work** (owner mandate) scaled to your roles in steps 1–2.

**The solo cheat (non-crew route) — past-posting.** The town's books
don't all close at the same minute: the handle posts at the track and
reaches the Punchline's desk first, but the gas station's book and the
corner store's jar stay open a little longer — the town runs on gossip
speed. The cheat: learn the number the moment it posts (be at the right
venue, or buy the tip from Silas), then **race the news across town**
and place a "bet" on a number that has already won before that book
closes. Travel itself is the exploit: bus timing, `roadside_map`,
weather, and route choice decide whether you beat the close. It is
found, not taught — a rumor chain and one Silas encounter seed the
knowledge. Getting caught past-posting doesn't summon the law — it
summons **Knuckles**: you cheated the street, and street debt is worse
than heat.

**The leak (owner mandate).** Any successful fix — yours or the crew's —
bleeds. Next day, rumor chatter around town starts carrying the number
("everybody's aunt is playing 3-1-8"): pile-on bets spike, the crew caps
payouts, and venues get twitchy. Fix it too often and the golden goose
gets loud enough that the sweep re-routes toward Numbers venues. The
town knowing things is the point of the town — including this.

## The Heist — four plan briefs (A + B ship in 0.6; C + D documented for future)

The heist is a crew-assisted Grand Casino finale parallel to the
existing duel, echoing its 4-phase structure: **The Plan → The Setup →
The Play → The Getaway.** Per owner lock: **plans are world-seed-gated**
— each plan generates into the run only when its world criteria align
(criteria are common enough that most crew runs see at least one plan,
but no plan is ever forced into a seed). The planning table shows which
plans the stars have aligned for this run, and what's missing for the
ones they haven't.

Every brief below answers the same five questions: what's the story,
what must the world provide, what do you actually *do* in Setup, what do
you actually *play* in The Play, and what's the end goal.

### Plan A — "The Count" (Bishop's plan) — the silent one

- **Story.** Bishop has spent years inside the Grand Casino being
  deferential and invisible, feeding numbers to the crew. On Audit
  Night the casino recounts itself: every cage drawer consolidates into
  the count room, and for one 40-minute window the money travels the
  back corridor in a cart. Bishop knows the window. He needs a face the
  cameras haven't learned — you.
- **World criteria (seed gate).** *Audit Night* seeded at the Grand
  Casino node + Bishop recruited to Inner Circle.
- **End goal.** Steal the consolidated count cart — the largest pure-cash
  payout in the game, taken without winning a single bet.
- **Setup (discrete objectives, done over prior turns).**
  1. *Become furniture*: play clean, boring sessions at the Grand Casino
     until the floor reads you as a regular — real gambling with a
     suspicion ceiling and bet-band constraints (win small, lose small,
     never spike).
  2. *The schedule*: hold position near the cage during a shift change
     so Bishop can lift the audit roster (a Streets-framework "hold"
     job, indoors).
  3. *The swap cart*: Mags builds a duplicate; you deliver it to the
     loading dock (a hard Streets run ending inside the casino's
     service perimeter).
  4. *(Optional)* Knuckles buys the corridor guard's marker at Debt
     Court — on the night, that guard takes a walk.
- **The Play.** You are the alibi and the trigger. You must hold a live,
  visibly boring table session while the phase timer runs — the game
  cuts between your hands and the crew's beats. At three decision
  points you choose: call the go early or hold (cart position vs.
  camera loop timing), cause a small deliberate incident to cover
  Switch's camera reset (intentional minor heat you must then sit on),
  and choose the cart's exit (dock = fast/loud, corridor = slow/quiet)
  based on what Setup you actually completed. Gambling-as-performance:
  your bets are watched the whole time, and a heat spike mid-window
  blows the corridor.
- **Getaway.** Rook, dock side. Outcome ladder applies.
- **Tone.** Procedural silence. The house's courtesy turned inside out —
  you steal the thing the velvet was hiding.

### Plan B — "The Whale Game" (Velvet & Mags' plan) — the loud one

- **Story.** Rourke hosts a private invitational for whales — a polite
  fleecing with velvet manners, where the house's edge is not entirely
  natural. Velvet can seat you at it under a borrowed name with a
  whale's vouch. Mags can arm you better than the house: their cooler
  against her cold deck, their dice against her loads. The house thinks
  it's shearing another rich idiot. The rich idiot is armed.
- **World criteria (seed gate).** A whale present in the run (*Whale
  Aboard* on the Delta Queen or *Gala Night* at the Grand Casino) +
  Velvet at Inner Circle. The whale scenario is where you earn the
  vouch.
- **End goal.** Win the invitational's cage-backed pot at the table —
  beat the house at its own crooked game, in front of Rourke.
- **Setup.**
  1. *The vouch*: befriend the whale inside his scenario — stake-horse
     his table, lose to him charmingly (deliberate-loss gambling with
     targets), keep his entourage amused.
  2. *The rig*: source Mags' components (pawn shop / Estate Lot chain)
     and pass the Practice Rig's skill check — the dice-setting trainer
     gates whether the loaded-dice line is live on the night.
  3. *The name*: build the borrowed identity with Velvet — spend and be
     seen (bankroll display minimums, right venues, right nights).
  4. *The drunk*: Lucky gets seated at the table's far end as loud
     cover.
- **The Play.** A real multi-round, high-stakes private table — rotating
  craps and card rounds against house players — with the **full
  skill-cheat system live under maximum scrutiny**. The twist: the
  house's own rig is in play, and Mags' counter-rig only works if you
  play it correctly (cheat timing windows, when to let a rigged hand
  *lose* to stay clean). Rourke's personal attention is the suspicion
  meter. Coordinated plays are your limited lifelines. You can lose
  this heist by losing the game — the only plan where the score is
  won with chips.
- **Getaway.** You walk out the front door with a receipt. The getaway
  is social: cashing out that big, that fast, without the borrowed name
  cracking (a compressed cage/interview beat), then Rook.
- **Tone.** Loud, social, performative — the street wearing the house's
  courtesy as a mask, one sentence at a time.

### Plan C — "The Jackpot Job" (Switch & Mags' plan) — the caper

- **Story.** The Grand Casino's flagship progressive bank — the Colossus
  — has been fattened all run by the whole town (its meter is town-state,
  fed turn by turn by everyone's play). Switch can reach its service
  loop during a maintenance window. Mags can teach it a trigger
  sequence. But a jackpot that size doesn't get stolen — it gets
  **claimed**: paperwork, a handshake photo, and Vivienne's smiling
  interview. The crew doesn't rob the machine; it manufactures a
  winner. The winner is you.
- **World criteria (seed gate).** Colossus meter above a threshold
  (town-fed — the player can *push* it there by seeding play and
  rumors) + a service-window scenario at the Grand Casino (*Convention
  Crowd* or a maintenance beat) + Switch at Inner Circle.
- **End goal.** Trigger and legally claim the progressive — the
  cleanest-looking dirtiest money in the game, walked out the front
  door with a tax form.
- **Setup.**
  1. *Fatten the pig*: push the meter over threshold — play the bank,
     spread "the Colossus is due" rumors so the town piles on (the
     rumor system as a tool you aim).
  2. *The window*: get Switch four uninterrupted minutes at the service
     panel — you run interference on floor staff (an indoor Streets
     "hold and divert" sequence with two staged distractions).
  3. *The sequence*: Mags programs the trigger; a component exists only
     in the pawn shop's Estate Lot (chain crossover).
  4. *The lucky idiot*: build the opposite of Plan A's identity —
     visible, sloppy, loud sessions so the floor already believes
     you're the kind of fool who'd hit it.
- **The Play.** Two acts. First, the trigger: at the rigged machine,
  enter Mags' sequence *through real spins* — a timing/input minigame
  layered on the slot surface while an attendant drifts nearby
  (mistime = burned spin; three burns = sequence resets and heat
  ticks). Then the hit — sirens, lights, crowd — and the verification
  gauntlet: Vivienne's interview, a dialogue sequence where your
  answers are checked against **what you actually did in the run**
  (where you slept, what you played, who saw you — the living town as
  your alibi or your undoing).
- **Getaway.** There isn't one. That's the joke. You leave through the
  front door while the house applauds. Rook drives you home anyway;
  he doesn't trust applause.
- **Tone.** Caper-comedy. The machine age turned against the house that
  owns it.

### Plan D — "Lights Out" (Knuckles & Switch's plan) — the nerve test

- **Story.** The grid is failing this season (Rolling Blackout
  happening) and the Grand Casino's backup has a seam: between outage
  and generator spin-up there are ~90 dark seconds — cameras rebooting,
  doors on manual, cages sealed but **table floats and the high-limit
  chip bank exposed**. Knuckles has wanted this one for years. Nobody
  before ever had someone inside the rope when the lights died.
- **World criteria (seed gate).** *Rolling Blackout* seeded as a town
  happening + Knuckles at Inner Circle. You don't schedule this plan —
  you ride the town's chaos, which is the pitch and the problem.
- **End goal.** Strip as much of the floats and the high-limit bank as
  nerve allows across the blackout seams, then convert the hot chips
  before the house rotates serials.
- **Setup.**
  1. *Chart the seams*: be present in venues during two earlier outage
     windows to time the dark (world-reading gameplay — the blackout
     schedule is legible only by living through it).
  2. *The doors*: Knuckles places crew on two manual doors.
  3. *The bags*: pre-stage caches near the casino (Streets runs).
  4. *The rehearsal*: the crew builds a floor mock-up in the Punchline's
     layer 2 and you run the grab route blind — a practice version of
     the actual Play board; your rehearsal time sets your efficiency on
     the night.
- **The Play.** Three blackout windows, each a timed grab-route on a
  darkened casino floor board (the Streets framework brought indoors):
  every float has a value/noise/weight profile, every grab extends your
  route, and the window closes whether you're clear or not. Between
  windows the lights are UP and you must be innocently mid-hand at a
  table (compressed gambling interludes under a suspicion ceiling,
  holding a bag). The core mechanic is the greed knob: the plan cannot
  tell you when to stop. Then the epilogue: hot chips must be converted
  over the following turns (cage visits under thresholds, table
  wash-throughs) before serial rotation — a laundering mini-arc where
  the town's other casinos suddenly matter.
- **Getaway.** The third window *is* the getaway — Rook kills the
  service-entrance light one beat early and you walk out through your
  own darkness.
- **Tone.** Nerve and appetite. The only plan where the town itself is
  the accomplice.

## The Turn — full design v2 (round-3 mandate: expanded)

### What it is

The Turn is the heist's betrayal outcome: one crew member sells the plan
out, and the score collapses in a plan-specific way (Plan A: the
corridor doesn't hold; Plan B: your rig gets made mid-game; Plan C: the
trigger sequence has been recoded; Plan D: a window doesn't reopen with
you inside the rope). It is the bottom rung of the outcome ladder — a
dramatic failure ending with its own copy and story scar, not a save
wipe.

### Who can turn (proposed answer to the pool question)

- **Rook is always loyal.** He recruited you; he is the path's anchor,
  and the Getaway phase logically requires an honest wheelman until the
  ladder resolves. If Rook could turn, no outcome except The Turn could
  ever feel safe.
- **The chosen plan's architect is always loyal** (Bishop for A, Velvet
  and Mags for B, Switch and Mags for C, Knuckles and Switch for D).
  Nobody torpedoes the plan they authored and starred in — and this
  gives players a legible deduction foothold: *choosing a plan clears
  its architects.*
- **Everyone else is eligible.** Between two and four suspects per plan,
  varying by which plan you chose — plan choice is itself a move in the
  trust game.

### How the traitor is chosen (deterministic, earned)

Every member carries a hidden **grievance ledger** — concrete, listable
debts you created: a job you abandoned, crew stake-horse money you lost
and shrugged at, a Distraction play where you let the member eat the
heat, a wrong accusation (below), a favor converted to cash and left
unpaid, and — the crossover with teeth — **past-posting the Numbers
while on the crew path** (cheating the street's own game while wearing
its colors is the biggest grievance in the book).

At the moment the plan is locked at the planning table ("the score gets
real"), the run RNG resolves The Turn: base chance near zero, weighted
up by the total grievance ledger of each eligible member. **Zero
grievances = zero chance.** That is hidden guarantee #1 — clean hands —
and it is never stated anywhere: the game does not tell you the ledger
exists.

### How you detect it (hidden guarantee #2 — discovery + skill)

If a member has turned, the run starts leaking exactly three classes of
clue, all surfaced through systems you have to actually play:

1. **The wrong tell.** Back-room poker gives every member an authored
   tell you can only learn by playing hands against them. After the
   plan locks, the traitor's tell misfires — it shows during heist
   table-talk, not during bluffs. You can only notice a tell being
   *wrong* if you learned it being *right*: the skill gate is real
   play, not a stat check.
2. **The itinerary lie.** The traitor claims to have been somewhere the
   living town can contradict — a rumor, a traveling NPC sighting, or
   your own eyes put them somewhere else. Only players who read the
   town catch it.
3. **The light envelope.** One of the traitor's job payouts comes up
   short in a specific, checkable way (the amount references something
   you can verify at the Numbers desk or the job board).

### The confrontation

Holding **at least two clues pointing at the same member** unlocks an
unmarked option at the planning table before departure — it never
appears otherwise, and nothing ever advertises that it can exist.

- **Right member, right evidence:** the Turn is cancelled and replaced
  with a loyalty beat — the member comes clean about the grievance, the
  crew tightens, and the heist gains a small edge (one free coordinated
  play during The Play).
- **Wrong member:** the accusation is itself a grievance — trust
  craters, the real traitor's odds spike, and the crew's mood at the
  table darkens. The guarantee cuts both ways; certainty is the skill.
- **Suspicion without proof:** with exactly one clue, you can still act
  — a hedge option lets you quietly restructure your role to survive
  the Turn (if it comes, you keep a partial haul and the *Out Hot*
  outcome instead of the full collapse). Insurance, not immunity.

### Discipline

No quest tracker, no clue journal UI, no "3/3 evidence" counter. Clues
live where you found them (the poker table, the streets, the envelope).
The game never admits the mechanism exists. Discovery belongs to the
community.

## Coin Pusher — a 3-variation family (names approved round 3)

Built like video poker and slots: one framework, three launch variations
that differ in mechanics, bonuses, and strategy. Shared framework: a
deterministic, budget-cheap pile simulation (seeded pile states, no
per-frame physics soup), drop-lane aim, shelf cycles, **pile persistence
in the node snapshot** (the pile you built is still there on revisit
unless tonight's scenario says someone else played it), and rumor
integration ("the pusher at the gas station is *hanging*").

### The universal nudge system (round-3 mandate)

**All three machines are nudgeable.** Nudging is one shared, physical
language across the family:

- **The nudge.** Choose force (tap / shove / slam) and direction (left
  / right / front-on), timed against the shelf cycle. A well-timed,
  well-aimed nudge at the right phase drops edge hangers **cleanly — no
  alarm** — this is the skill expression, and it must feel earned.
- **The alarm.** Every machine has a hidden tolerance, seeded per node
  and modified by venue security and tonight's scenario (*Graveyard
  Shift* maintenance = lax; *Serial-Check Day* nearby = twitchy).
  Pushing past tolerance trips the machine: the machine locks down for
  the night and a **significant heat spike** lands. There is no forced
  exit (round-4 amendment): the heat flows through the existing heat
  systems — floor-staff warnings, pit pressure, sweep interplay — and
  can escalate into a situation where the player *needs* to leave, but
  the choice and the squirming stay theirs. The node remembers
  (snapshot flag: staff watch you next visit).
- **The tell ladder.** Alarms don't come from nowhere: soft warnings
  first — the machine rocks, a chirp, the attendant's head turns
  (suspicion ticks) — so a careful player is always one readable step
  from the line, and a greedy one knows exactly what they're risking.
- **Smash-and-grab (legitimate strategy).** Deliberately overslam for a
  massive drop, scoop the tray, eat the alarm and its heat, and choose
  to leave at speed before the heat escalates into something worse — a
  planned withdrawal, viable **if your heat can take it**. Pairs
  naturally with the swept-window (post-sweep venues have nowhere to
  escalate) and with Rook waiting outside.

Per-variation nudge flavor: Quarter Falls nudges move the *pile*
(overhang collapses), Jackpot Ridge nudges move the *pucks* (its meter
is deepest — nudging is its native verb), The Vault Drop nudges move
*fragments* (and its alarm is the twitchiest — the vault protects
itself).

### Variation 1 — "Quarter Falls" (the timing pusher)
- **Core mechanic:** pure timing and topology. Two oscillating shelves,
  drop lanes with different approach angles, a readable pile with
  visible overhangs and edge hangers. Aim the lane, time the drop
  against the shelf cycle.
- **Bonus layer:** *prize riders* — items sit ON the pile (chip stacks,
  a watch, a ticket roll, rarely a real inventory item seeded by
  scenario) and ride the pile forward. Side-gutters eat greedy shots.
- **Strategy:** pile reading. When to feed the middle vs. attack an
  overhang, when a machine is "pregnant," and — because piles persist —
  when to *leave* a machine loaded for later, or race a rumor to
  somebody else's abandoned setup.
- **Cheat line:** `cold_quarters` (denser drops), `coin_return_shim`
  (gutter recovery).

### Variation 2 — "Jackpot Ridge" (the puck pusher)
- **Core mechanic:** the shelf carries **feature pucks**, not just
  coins: multiplier pucks (arm ×2/×3/×5 for the next N drops), lock
  pucks (freeze a shelf one cycle), dud pucks that jam a lane.
  Sequencing over timing: which puck comes off first decides the run.
- **Bonus layer:** *Ridge Runs* — landing three armed multipliers in one
  shelf cycle triggers a cascade where the back wall pushes double-time.
- **Strategy:** puzzle sequencing plus the deepest nudge game in the
  family — Ridge is where nudge mastery pays most and where the alarm
  line gets walked closest.
- **Cheat line:** `weighted_keyring` (heavier nudges within tolerance),
  a Mags-tuned dampener on the crew path (raises alarm tolerance one
  band).

### Variation 3 — "The Vault Drop" (the progressive pusher)
- **Core mechanic:** the pile carries **key fragments**. Fragments
  pushed off the ledge bank toward the machine's **vault** — a
  pick-and-reveal bonus round behind a door on the playfield. The vault
  holds a **progressive jackpot fed by the town**: town-state driven,
  growing turn by turn, faster in crowded scenarios.
- **Bonus layer:** the vault round — spend banked fragments on vault
  cells holding cash, items, fragment refunds, or the RESET cell that
  slams the progressive down. Stop anytime; greed is the boss.
- **Strategy:** EV reading across the *map*, not the machine. Which
  venue's Vault Drop is fat tonight (rumors and Switch tell you),
  whether to bank fragments or cash a thin vault before the sweep
  arrives, when the meter justifies a trip across town. The only
  machine in the game you play by *traveling*.
- **Cheat line:** `xray_glasses` — peek one vault cell before choosing.

**Placement:** distributed like slots across tier-1 venues (gas station,
corner store, bar), variation availability seeded per node with
scenario-dependent access (*Graveyard Shift* opens the maintenance
panel; *Trucker Convoy* means the good machine is occupied).
