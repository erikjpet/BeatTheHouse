## Devlog post

### Title

**Beat the House 0.6.0: The Town Remembers Tonight**

### Opening

Version 0.6.0 turns a run into a night that follows you. Rooms have their own
people, pressure, and aftereffects. The Crew can pull you into work that reaches
the Grand Casino. Eleven full games now sit inside a town built to remember
where you have been and what you did there.

The old debt-and-Heat spiral is still the heart of Beat the House. The
difference is that the places around it now feel occupied: dealers work their
tables, staff stay behind their counters, props belong to the room, and jobs
that are not physical objects live in a clear action list instead of floating
over the floor.

### The living town

Fifty-five persistent Tonight scenarios can change who is in a room, what is
happening there, which actions are available, and what remains when you return.
Weather, venue schedules, rumors, travelers, route trouble, and Police Sweeps
can bend the shape of a run without exposing the result in advance.

A rumor now arrives as an actual conversation with a useful player-facing cue.
People speak before their choices resolve, and they remain present until the
conversation reaches a real outcome. The town feels less like a set of menus
and more like a collection of places under pressure.

### The Crew path

The Crew is now a connected campaign. Build trust, take deliveries and Numbers
work, recruit specialists, join coordinated table plays, and deal with the
consequences when the police start leaning on the room. Follow the route far
enough and you can carry a Crew plan into the Grand Casino for a third way out.

The Crew remembers help, refusals, and grudges. Ignoring them is still a valid
choice; getting involved means accepting that later jobs may remember the way
you handled the earlier ones.

### New games and machines

The roster now covers eleven full game modules. Casino and street Craps offer
distinct tables and a wide wager set. Three deterministic Coin Pusher cabinets
have their own goals and live trays. The Crew's back-room game is six-handed
no-limit Hold'em with persistent opponents and readable personalities.

The existing games grew deeper too. Slots carry Pinball and Buffalo feature
families, table games have stronger dealing and settlement rituals, and Pull
Tabs can show a rare yellow glimmer on an eligible unrevealed ticket without
changing its fixed contents, odds, or payout.

### Three ways through the Grand Casino

Linda's clean route climbs the Bronze, Silver, and Gold Players Card ladder.
Rourke's route turns a walk, pat-down, interrogation, and five-hand heads-up
Blackjack duel into a pressure-cooker exit. The Crew route brings a heist plan
to the Grand Casino and carries its outcome into the run report.

Blackjack counting is clearer along the way: the table shows **YOUR COUNT**
only when the player is actively counting, and Cass waits for a current,
recorded shoe before count-dependent choices open.

### Depth across the game

Rooms now use fixed, named places for games, shop items, people, and illustrated
props. Staff stand behind counters, table games sit on felt, machines align with
the room art, and scenario actions stay readable without pretending to be
physical objects.

The guided first night and contextual lessons now cover the Living Town, Crew,
Craps, Coin Pusher, poker, and Police Sweep systems. Thirteen surface sound
profiles and adaptive music accents give games and room events more response,
while native and Web builds keep the same core audio mix.

### Under the surface

Persistent scenarios, Crew relationships, travel, game state, and ending
progress survive save and Continue. Seeded systems keep their outcomes
repeatable, and player-visible projections avoid exposing private results before
the game reveals them. Windows and Web remain the supported 0.6.0 release
targets; the Web build uses a single-threaded delivery path.

### Long description

**The town remembers tonight.**

Beat the House is a single-player casino roguelike about surviving a debt-and-
Heat spiral across low-stakes rooms and the Grand Casino. Build a seeded run,
buy risky items, take loans and services, travel between venues, and decide how
far you are willing to push your luck.

**A living town**

Fifty-five persistent Tonight scenarios bring changing people, pressure,
rumors, routes, Police Sweeps, and aftermath to the city's rooms. Return visits
remember what happened. Fixed, art-aligned layouts keep staff, games, shop
items, and props grounded in the places where they belong.

**The Crew path**

Build trust through deliveries, Numbers work, recruitment, and coordinated
table plays. The Crew remembers favors and grudges, and one route can carry a
heist plan into the Grand Casino. You can also leave the Crew alone and build
your run another way.

**Eleven full games**

Play Scratch Tickets, Pull Tabs, Slots, Coin Pusher, Bar Dice, Craps,
Blackjack, Baccarat, Roulette, Video Poker, and the Crew's back-room Hold'em
game. Each game owns its own surface, rules, actions, animation, and
consequences inside the larger run.

**Three Grand Casino endings**

Climb Linda's Players Card ladder and leave Gold. Walk into Rourke's back room
and survive the house's pressure. Or bring the Crew's plan to the casino and
live with the result. Every route grows out of the same run: your bankroll,
Heat, access, relationships, and choices.

**Built for repeatable runs**

Seeded generation, persistent rooms, save and Continue support, eleven deep
game modules, expanded lessons, stronger room presentation, and responsive
sound make each route readable without giving away what is still hidden.

Beat the House uses simulated gambling only. There is no real-money wagering,
no cash prize, and no gambling monetization.
